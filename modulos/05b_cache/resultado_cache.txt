CONCLUSOES E LIMITES
- The kernels are synthetic (copy, 40x40 matrix, sorting, linked list, application with 120 functions). There is no real R8064 program trace: the HIT_I/HIT_D rates hold for these examples, not for your programs.
- 8 KB (I) + 8 KB (D) cache, 32 B line, 2 ways: average gain of 2.26x over the current spreadsheet model and 1.65x over the baseline with prefetch queue (module 05 SDRAM controller).
  With the overlapped-read controller (estimate, not simulated): 2.62x and 1.91x. Cost: 8 data BSRAM blocks (+ tags in SSRAM/LUT, ~1-2 blocks): from ~16/46 to ~26/46.
- The gain comes mainly from instructions: small loops fit entirely (HIT_I ~1.0) and fetch stops paying 4 clocks per instruction. For data, the gain depends on locality: sorting 0.99, matrix 0.87, copy 0.75 (spatial locality only), linked list 0.50.
- A 64 B line only helps when there is spatial locality (copy, matrix) and makes the linked list WORSE (64 B fill to use 16 B) and the application; for general code, 32 B with 2 ways.
- Write-through: writes still cost the spreadsheet wait. A write buffer or write-back cache would improve it; it was not modeled. Coherence with the MMU/Z80/MSX (DMA on the same SDRAM) was NOT addressed: the cache needs to be invalidated on classic Z80 and MSX interface accesses or stay only in the R8064 region.
- Assumptions: hit = 0 wait (synchronous BSRAM; address in F1/X, data in F2/M); 80% of fills find the SDRAM page open; fill of the whole line before restarting (no critical word first).
- For the spreadsheet: HIT_I and HIT_D are a single number each; reasonable values for 8 KB + 8 KB: HIT_I ~0.95 and HIT_D ~0.85 for general code (application: 0.94 and 0.91); but the spreadsheet cycle model does NOT yet distinguish the miss cost (fill) from the current wait: the miss formula would have to be added.
- NOT simulated in RTL: the cache RTL, its timing at 100 MHz (tag compare in stage F1/M) and the LUT area. It could be the next step.
